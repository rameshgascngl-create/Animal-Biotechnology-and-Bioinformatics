Hybridoma Technology and Monoclonal Antibodies

Hybridoma Technology and Monoclonal Antibodies

Antibodies are the immune system's precision tools. By fusing an antibody-producing cell with an immortal tumour cell, Köhler and Milstein created a way to make unlimited quantities of a single, perfectly specific antibody.

📗 Chapter 1

Antibodies: Polyclonal versus Monoclonal

Monoclonal antibodies are identical antibodies from a single B-cell clone that recognise one epitope.

An antibody (immunoglobulin) is a Y-shaped glycoprotein made by plasma cells. It binds an antigen at a specific site called the epitope.

Fig. 1. Structure of an IgG antibody.
FeaturePolyclonal antibodiesMonoclonal antibodies (MAbs)
SourceMany B-cell clonesOne B-cell clone (hybridoma)
Epitope recognisedMany different epitopesOne epitope
SpecificityLower; cross-reactivityVery high
Batch consistencyVaries between animalsUniform, unlimited supply
ProductionImmunise animal and collect serumHybridoma culture
Monoclonal antibody
A population of identical antibodies, all produced by a single clone of B lymphocytes and directed against a single antigenic determinant.

Historical landmark

Georges Köhler and César Milstein (Cambridge, 1975) fused antibody-producing B cells with myeloma cells to make immortal antibody-secreting hybridomas. They shared the 1984 Nobel Prize in Physiology or Medicine with Niels Jerne.

Hybridoma Technology: Step by Step

Fuse a mortal antibody-making cell with an immortal cancer cell to get a cell that does both jobs.

A hybrid cell formed by fusion of a B lymphocyte (which makes a specific antibody but dies in culture) with a myeloma cell (immortal but makes no antibody). It secretes monoclonal antibody and divides indefinitely.

Remember

Aminopterin blocks the de novo pathway; hypoxanthine and thymidine feed the salvage pathway, which needs HGPRT. Only cells with HGPRT can use that rescue.

💡
Alternative: phage display▼
Gene fragments for antibody variable regions are displayed on bacteriophage and selected without immunising animals. George Smith and Gregory Winter shared the 2018 Nobel Prize in Chemistry; adalimumab was the first fully human antibody developed this way.
⚠
Problems with mouse antibodies▼
Humans make HAMA (human anti-mouse antibodies), which clear the drug and may cause allergic reactions. This led to chimeric, humanised and fully human antibodies.

Engineered Monoclonal Antibodies

Genetic engineering reduces the mouse content of therapeutic antibodies.

CDRs

The antigen-binding specificity lies in the complementarity-determining regions (CDRs) of the variable domains. Humanisation grafts only these loops onto a human antibody scaffold.

📒 Chapter 4

Applications of Monoclonal Antibodies

MAbs are used in therapy, diagnosis, purification and research.

🧬

Cancer therapy

  • Trastuzumab (Herceptin) — HER2-positive breast cancer.
  • Rituximab — anti-CD20, B-cell lymphoma (1997, first cancer MAb).
  • Pembrolizumab — anti-PD-1 checkpoint inhibitor.
🧬

Autoimmune disease

  • Adalimumab and infliximab block TNF-α in rheumatoid arthritis, Crohn's disease.
🧪

Diagnosis

  • Pregnancy test strips (anti-hCG).
  • ELISA and rapid antigen tests.
  • Blood grouping, tumour markers (PSA).
🔬

Research & purification

  • Immunoaffinity chromatography.
  • Western blotting, immunofluorescence, flow cytometry.
  • Tissue typing and cell sorting.
💉

Transplantation

  • Anti-CD3 (OKT3) to prevent graft rejection.
💪

Imaging

  • Radiolabelled MAbs for tumour imaging.

Limitations

  • Expensive to produce.
  • Immunogenicity of non-human antibodies.
  • Poor penetration into solid tumours.
  • Hybridomas can lose chromosomes and stop secreting.

📋 Summary

Summary & Exam Revision

Key points and model answers likely to appear in university examinations.

Key exam points
1
Hybridoma = B lymphocyte + myeloma cell; Köhler & Milstein, 1975; Nobel 1984.
2
Myeloma cells are HGPRT-deficient; HAT medium selects hybrids.
3
Fusing agent: PEG. Screening: ELISA. Cloning: limiting dilution.
4
Mouse antibodies cause HAMA; solution: chimeric (-ximab), humanised (-zumab), human (-umab).
5
Phage display (Smith and Winter, Nobel 2018) produces fully human MAbs.
6
Uses: cancer therapy, autoimmune disease, diagnostics, purification, research.
Short-answer questions

Click a question to reveal the model answer.

Q.1What are monoclonal antibodies? How are they different from polyclonal antibodies?▼

MAbs are identical antibodies from a single B-cell clone, specific for one epitope. Polyclonal antibodies come from many clones and recognise many epitopes.

Q.2Describe the production of monoclonal antibodies.▼

Immunise mouse → collect spleen B cells → fuse with HGPRT-deficient myeloma cells using PEG → select hybridomas in HAT medium → screen supernatants by ELISA → clone by limiting dilution → large-scale culture or ascites → purification.

Q.3Explain the role of HAT medium.▼

Aminopterin blocks de novo nucleotide synthesis, so cells must use the HGPRT salvage pathway with hypoxanthine and thymidine. HGPRT-deficient myeloma cells die, B cells die naturally, and hybridomas survive.

Q.4Give four applications of MAbs.▼

Cancer therapy (trastuzumab, rituximab); diagnosis (pregnancy test, ELISA); immunoaffinity purification; prevention of graft rejection (OKT3); imaging.

Q.5Why are humanised antibodies preferred?▼

They reduce the HAMA response, have longer half-lives and are safer for repeated administration.

📖 Glossary

Key Terms & Glossary

Important terms in simple language.

Antibody
Y-shaped protein that binds a specific antigen.
Epitope
The part of an antigen recognised by an antibody.
Hybridoma
Immortal antibody-secreting cell formed by B cell and myeloma fusion.
HAT medium
Hypoxanthine-Aminopterin-Thymidine medium that selects hybridomas.
HGPRT
Hypoxanthine-guanine phosphoribosyl transferase, enzyme of the salvage pathway.
Ascites
Fluid in the mouse peritoneum that accumulates MAb-secreting hybridoma cells.
CDR
Complementarity-determining region of the antibody variable domain.
HAMA
Human anti-mouse antibody immune response.
Phage display
Technique displaying peptides or antibody fragments on phage for selection.
Fc region
Constant tail of an antibody that engages immune effector functions.
