PG focus

  • Dolly’s mitochondrial DNA came from the egg donor, so she was a nuclear clone, not a complete genetic copy.

Animals as Bioreactors, Superbug and Bioremediation

Transgenic animals can make drugs in milk; engineered microbes can clean up pollutants; both raise biosafety questions.

Animal bioreactor: a transgenic animal that secretes a therapeutic protein into milk, blood, egg white or urine. Milk is preferred because yields are high (g/L) and proteins are glycosylated like human proteins.

Bioweapons (concept only)

  • The 1972 Biological Weapons Convention (in force 1975) bans development, production and stockpiling of biological agents as weapons.
  • CDC Category A agents (highest priority) include anthrax, smallpox, plague, botulinum toxin, tularaemia and viral haemorrhagic fevers.
  • Response relies on surveillance, vaccines, antimicrobials and biosafety/biosecurity rules.

Applications of DNA fingerprinting (Alec Jeffreys, 1984)

  • Forensics: identity from blood, semen, hair roots.

Summary & Exam Revision

Key points and model answers likely to appear in university examinations.

Key exam points
1
Transgene = promoter + coding sequence + poly(A); microinjection into the male pronucleus.
2
Knockout: ES cells, neo/tk selection, blastocyst injection, chimera, breed; 1:2:1 in F2.
3
Dolly (1996): SCNT from an adult mammary cell; 277 fusions gave 1 live lamb.
4

Superbug (Chakrabarty): Pseudomonas with 4 plasmids; Diamond v. Chakrabarty (1980).

Hybridoma: B cell + HGPRT− myeloma; HAT selects; screen, clone, expand.
7

Ex vivo vs in vivo gene therapy; CAR-T is ex vivo; oncolytic viruses and suicide genes.

10

Short-answer questions

Click a question to reveal the model answer.

Q.1Describe the production of a transgenic mouse.▼

A construct (promoter + gene + poly A) is injected into the male pronucleus of fertilised eggs; embryos are transferred to a pseudopregnant foster; pups are screened by PCR/Southern; founders are bred to confirm germ-line transmission.

Q.2How is a knockout mouse produced?▼

A targeting vector with a neo cassette replaces an exon in ES cells by homologous recombination; neo and tk selection enriches targeted cells; they are injected into blastocysts to give chimeras; breeding gives heterozygotes and then homozygous knockouts.

Q.3Explain the cloning of Dolly.▼

A mammary cell from an adult ewe was starved to G0 and fused by electrical pulse with an enucleated egg; the embryo was cultured and transferred to a surrogate; 1 of 277 fusions produced a live lamb in 1996.

Q.4What is the role of HAT medium?▼

Aminopterin blocks de novo nucleotide synthesis, so cells must use the salvage pathway (HGPRT, TK). HGPRT-minus myeloma cells die; unfused B cells die naturally; only hybrids survive.

Q.5Differentiate ex vivo and in vivo gene therapy.▼

Ex vivo: patient cells are modified outside the body and returned. In vivo: the gene vector is injected directly into the patient.

Q.6Write short notes on CRISPR-Cas9.▼

A guide RNA directs Cas9 to a 20-nt target adjacent to an NGG PAM; Cas9 makes a double-strand break; NHEJ gives knockouts and HDR gives precise edits.

Q.7Distinguish RFLP and RAPD.▼

RFLP detects fragment-length differences using restriction enzymes and a probe (co-dominant, reproducible). RAPD uses random primers in PCR (dominant, quick, less reproducible).

📖 Glossary

Key Terms & Glossary

Important terms in simple language.

Transgene
A foreign gene inserted into an organism.
Founder (F0)
First transgenic animal produced.
ES cell
Embryonic stem cell, pluripotent and from the inner cell mass.
Chimera
Animal containing cells from two different embryos.
SCNT
Somatic cell nuclear transfer.
HGPRT
Hypoxanthine-guanine phosphoribosyl transferase of the salvage pathway.
Epitope
Part of an antigen recognised by an antibody.
Ex vivo
Treatment performed outside the body on removed cells.
PAM
Protospacer adjacent motif needed by Cas9.
NHEJ
Non-homologous end joining; error-prone DNA repair.
HDR
Homology-directed repair using a template.
STR
Short tandem repeat used in DNA profiling.
