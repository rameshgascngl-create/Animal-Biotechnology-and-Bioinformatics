Gel Electrophoresis

Principle · Types · Components · Procedure · Applications

How do scientists sort millions of DNA fragments by size after a restriction digest or PCR reaction? Gel electrophoresis — the fundamental technique that uses an electric field to separate molecules through a molecular sieve — is the answer. It underpins virtually every molecular biology workflow in use today.

  • Define gel electrophoresis and explain its importance in molecular biology.
  • Explain separation based on size, charge, and molecular sieving.
  • Describe all components and types of gel electrophoresis systems.

Section 1

🔬 Introduction to Gel Electrophoresis

Definition

Gel electrophoresis is a laboratory technique used to separate macromolecules — primarily DNA, RNA, and proteins — based on their size and electrical charge by causing them to migrate through a porous gel matrix under the influence of an electric field.

Importance in Molecular Biology and Biotechnology

  • DNA analysis: Separates fragments after restriction enzyme digestion or PCR amplification.
  • RNA analysis: Assesses RNA integrity and size during Northern blotting.
  • Protein analysis: Determines protein molecular weight and purity (SDS-PAGE).
  • Forensics: DNA fingerprinting by STR profiling for individual identification.
  • Diagnostics: Detection of viral/bacterial nucleic acids; identification of genetic mutations.
  • Clone verification: Confirms correct gene insertion into cloning vectors.

Basic Concept

When an electric field is applied, charged molecules migrate through a gel. The gel acts as a molecular sieve — smaller molecules pass through pores more easily and migrate farther, while larger molecules are retarded and move less. This creates distinct bands at positions corresponding to molecule size.

Golden Rule: Smaller molecules → faster migration → farther from well. Larger molecules → slower migration → closer to well.
Section 2

⚡ Principle of Gel Electrophoresis

Movement of Charged Molecules in an Electric Field

All DNA and RNA molecules carry a net negative charge at neutral pH due to the phosphate groups in their backbone. When an electric field is applied, these negatively charged molecules migrate towards the positive electrode (anode), moving away from the negative electrode (cathode).

Role of Size and Charge

  • Charge: All DNA fragments carry the same charge-to-mass ratio (uniform negative phosphate backbone) → charge alone does NOT separate them.
  • Size: The primary separating factor. Smaller fragments experience less resistance from the gel matrix and migrate faster.
  • Shape: Supercoiled, relaxed circular, and linear DNA of the same size migrate differently.

DNA Migration Towards the Anode

DNA samples are loaded into wells at the cathode (−) end. The applied voltage drives the negatively charged DNA backbone through the gel towards the anode (+) end. Wells are always positioned at the negative electrode end.

Molecular Sieving

The gel matrix forms a mesh-like network of pores:

  • Small fragments (e.g., 100 bp) → navigate pores easily → move quickly → travel far from well.
  • Large fragments (e.g., 10 kb) → physically impeded by pores → move slowly → remain close to well.

Fragment Size vs Migration Distance

There is a logarithmic (inverse) relationship between size and migration distance: log(bp) is inversely proportional to distance from well. Plotting log(fragment size) vs migration distance gives a straight line — used to estimate unknown sizes by comparison with a DNA ladder.

Exam Formula: log(fragment size in bp) ∝ −(migration distance from well)
Smaller bp → greater distance.  |  Larger bp → smaller distance.
Section 3

🗂️ Types of Gel Electrophoresis

A. Agarose Gel Electrophoresis

  • Matrix: Agarose — polysaccharide from red algae.
  • Concentration: 0.5–3% (higher % = smaller pores = better small-fragment resolution).
  • Molecules: DNA (50 bp–50 kb) and RNA. Orientation: horizontal.
  • Buffer: TAE (Tris-Acetate-EDTA) or TBE (Tris-Borate-EDTA).
  • Applications: PCR analysis, restriction mapping, clone verification, Southern blotting.

B. Polyacrylamide Gel Electrophoresis (PAGE)

  • Matrix: Polyacrylamide (synthetic polymer from acrylamide + bis-acrylamide).
  • Concentration: 4–20% with very small, tunable pore sizes.
  • Resolution: Very high; can resolve fragments differing by 1 bp (DNA sequencing gels).
  • Applications: Protein analysis (native PAGE), DNA sequencing, small DNA fragments (10–500 bp).
  • Safety: Acrylamide monomer is a neurotoxin — handle with care.

C. SDS-PAGE

  • Principle: SDS (sodium dodecyl sulphate) coats proteins with uniform negative charge proportional to their length → separation purely by molecular weight.
  • Use: Protein molecular weight determination; purity assessment.
  • Applications: Western blotting, proteomic analysis, recombinant protein quality control.
Agarose % Rule of Thumb: 0.5–0.8% for large DNA (2–50 kb) · 1% for general use (500 bp–10 kb) · 2–3% for small fragments (50–500 bp)
Section 4

🧰 Components of the Gel Electrophoresis System

1. Gel MatrixAgarose or polyacrylamide. Pore size controlled by concentration. Cast as a slab with wells. Acts as the molecular sieve that separates fragments by size.
2. Buffer (TAE/TBE)Maintains pH and ionic strength. Conducts electricity. EDTA in buffer chelates Mg²⁺ to inhibit DNase activity. Must submerge the entire gel in the chamber.
3. DNA SamplePurified DNA to be analysed (PCR products, restriction digests, genomic DNA). Mixed with loading dye before loading. Typically 50–500 ng per lane.
4. Loading DyeContains: glycerol/sucrose (increases density → sample sinks into well) + tracking dyes (bromophenol blue ≈ 300 bp; xylene cyanol ≈ 4 kb) to monitor migration.
5. DNA LadderPre-mixed fragments of precisely known sizes (e.g., 100 bp, 500 bp, 1 kb…). Loaded in a dedicated lane. Used to estimate sizes of unknown sample fragments by comparison.
6. Electrophoresis ChamberHorizontal tank (agarose) or vertical apparatus (PAGE). Contains electrode connections and buffer. Holds gel in place with cathode at well end and anode at far end.
7. Power SupplyProvides DC electric current (70–120 V typical). Higher voltage = faster migration but more heat. Connected: cathode (−) at wells, anode (+) at far end.
8. Staining AgentsEthidium bromide (EtBr): intercalates DNA, fluoresces orange under UV — MUTAGENIC. Safer alternatives: SYBR Green, GelRed, GelGreen. Protein gels: Coomassie Blue or silver stain.
Safety: Ethidium bromide is a potent mutagen and carcinogen. Always wear gloves, work in designated EtBr area, dispose of waste properly. SYBR Green and GelRed are widely used safer alternatives.
Section 5

🪜 Steps in Agarose Gel Electrophoresis

1
Preparation of the Gel

Agarose (e.g., 1 g for 1% in 100 mL TAE) is dissolved by heating until fully clear. Cooled to ~55°C, EtBr or safe alternative added, then poured into a casting tray with a comb. Gel solidifies at room temperature in 20–30 minutes forming a firm, translucent slab. Comb is removed to leave the wells.

2
Sample Preparation and Loading

Gel is placed in the electrophoresis chamber flooded with TAE buffer (just covering the gel). DNA samples are mixed with 6× loading dye (1 part dye : 5 parts DNA). Using a micropipette, the DNA ladder is loaded into lane 1, then samples into subsequent wells — carefully to avoid disturbing adjacent wells.

3
Electrophoresis (Applying Current)

The lid is secured and electrodes connected: red (+) to anode end, black (−) to cathode end (well end). Voltage set to 70–120 V DC. Current is applied. The tracking dyes in loading buffer (visible blue and purple bands) allow monitoring of migration progress.

4
Migration of DNA Fragments

DNA fragments migrate from the cathode (well) end towards the anode end at speeds inversely proportional to size. Run continues until the bromophenol blue dye has migrated approximately 75–80% of the gel length — typically 30–60 minutes at 100 V.

5
Staining and Visualisation

If EtBr was added to the gel during casting, the gel can be directly placed on a UV transilluminator (302 nm). EtBr-stained DNA fluoresces bright orange. If staining was not done beforehand, the gel is soaked in EtBr solution (0.5 µg/mL) for 20–30 minutes. Images are captured with a gel documentation (gel doc) system.

6
Interpretation of Bands

Each band = a population of same-sized DNA fragments. Band position (distance from well) is compared to ladder bands to estimate size. Band intensity is proportional to DNA amount. A log(bp) vs distance graph is plotted using ladder bands; sample sizes are interpolated from this line.

Section 7 — Migration Visual

🏃 Fragment Size vs Migration Speed

Fragments of different sizes migrating through the same gel at the same time.

Why does size matter? The agarose gel is a fixed mesh of pores. Large DNA fragments must squeeze through multiple pores at once — they are physically retarded. Small fragments slip through easily. This differential resistance to migration is the entire basis of molecular sieving in gel electrophoresis.

Section 9

🏥 Applications of Gel Electrophoresis

PCR Verification
Confirms correct amplicon size after PCR. Detects primer dimers, non-specific bands, and failed reactions.
Restriction Mapping
Separates restriction digest fragments to determine their sizes and map gene organisation.
DNA Fingerprinting
STR (short tandem repeat) profiling by multiplex PCR. Used in forensics, paternity testing, and individual identification.
Gene Cloning Analysis
Colony PCR and restriction digestion confirm correct insert size in recombinant vectors.
Mutation Detection
RFLP analysis, deletion/insertion screening. Different mutation profiles produce different band patterns.
Forensic Applications
Biological evidence from crime scenes. Disaster victim identification. Immigration casework.
Protein Analysis (SDS-PAGE)
Molecular weight determination. Purity assessment of recombinant proteins. Western blot preparation.
Clinical Diagnostics
Viral/bacterial DNA detection. RNA integrity assessment for transcriptomic studies. Pathogen drug resistance genotyping.
Section 10

⚖️ Advantages and Limitations

✅ Advantages

  • Simple and rapid: Results in 1–2 hours.
  • Versatile: Separates DNA, RNA, and proteins.
  • Semi-quantitative: Band intensity reflects DNA amount.
  • Size estimation: Ladder enables accurate sizing.
  • Multiple samples: 20–30 lanes per gel.
  • Inexpensive: Low cost reagents; reusable equipment.
  • PAGE resolution: Can resolve single base pair differences.

⚠️ Limitations

  • Size range: Agarose limited to 50 bp–50 kb.
  • EtBr hazard: Mutagenic; requires safe handling.
  • Co-migration: Very similar sizes (<10% difference) may overlap.
  • Shape effect: Circular vs linear DNA of same size migrate differently.
  • Not quantitative: Cannot give absolute copy numbers (needs qPCR).
  • Irreversible: Gel cannot be reused once run.
Common Errors: Smearing = DNA degradation (DNase) or overloading. No bands = failed PCR, wrong buffer, or UV bulb failure. Extra bands = non-specific amplification or contamination. Smile effect (curved bands) = overheating → reduce voltage.

Core Principle

  • Gel = molecular sieve → smaller fragments navigate pores faster → migrate farther.

  • log(bp) ∝ −distance from well → straight line on semi-log plot.

Exam tip: Smaller = farther from well = bottom of gel. Larger = closer to well = top of gel. Always remember this direction.
8 Components
  • Gel matrix (agarose/polyacrylamide): molecular sieve.
  • Buffer (TAE/TBE): maintains pH, conducts current, EDTA inhibits DNase.
  • Loading dye: glycerol (density) + tracking dyes (bromophenol blue ≈ 300 bp).
  • DNA ladder: known size reference for estimating unknown fragments.
  • EtBr: intercalates into DNA; fluoresces orange under UV — MUTAGENIC.
  • Power supply: 70–120 V DC; drives migration.
Exam tip: Know the function of loading dye — glycerol prevents sample floating out of well; bromophenol blue (visible blue dye) monitors migration (~300 bp equivalent); xylene cyanol tracks larger fragments (~4 kb).
6 Steps in Order
  1. Prepare agarose gel in TAE buffer → pour → solidify with comb.
  2. Place gel in chamber with TAE buffer.
  3. Mix DNA samples with loading dye; load ladder + samples into wells.
  4. Apply 70–120 V; run until dye front is 75–80% down gel.
  5. Visualise under UV (EtBr stained gel → orange fluorescent bands).
  6. Photograph and interpret bands using DNA ladder as size reference.
Exam tip: The direction is ALWAYS: wells at cathode (−) end → DNA moves away from wells towards anode (+) end. Voltage applied: red wire (+) to anode, black wire (−) to cathode.
Gel Analysis
  • Each band = population of DNA fragments of the same size.
  • Band position → compare with ladder → estimate fragment size in bp.
  • Band intensity → proportional to amount of DNA in that band.
  • Number of bands → number of distinct-sized fragments in sample.
  • Plot log(bp) vs migration distance using ladder → straight line → read unknown sizes off line.
Exam tip: After gel electrophoresis, bands of interest can be cut out of the gel with a scalpel and the DNA extracted by gel extraction kit. This purified DNA can then be used for cloning, sequencing, or other downstream applications.

Click-to-Reveal Key Terms

Agarose
Click to reveal
Polysaccharide from red algae (Gelidium/Gracilaria). Forms a porous gel when dissolved in buffer and cooled. Pore size is inversely proportional to concentration. 0.5% for large DNA (1–50 kb); 2–3% for small fragments (50–500 bp). The standard matrix for DNA and RNA electrophoresis.
Electrophoresis
Click to reveal
Movement of charged molecules through a medium under an applied electric field. Negatively charged DNA migrates towards the anode (+). Positive molecules migrate towards cathode (−). In gel electrophoresis, the gel matrix introduces size-dependent sieving.
DNA Ladder
Click to reveal
A pre-mixed standard containing DNA fragments of precisely known sizes (e.g., 100 bp, 500 bp, 1 kb, 5 kb). Run in a dedicated lane alongside samples. Band positions provide a standard curve (log bp vs distance) for estimating sizes of unknown fragments by interpolation.
Ethidium Bromide
Click to reveal
Planar fluorescent molecule that intercalates between DNA base pairs. When illuminated with UV light (302 nm), EtBr-DNA complexes fluoresce bright orange. Detects as little as 1–10 ng DNA per band. IMPORTANT: EtBr is a potent mutagen and carcinogen. Safer alternatives: SYBR Green, GelRed, GelGreen.
Loading Dye
Click to reveal
Solution mixed with DNA before loading into wells. Contains: (1) glycerol or Ficoll to increase sample density so it sinks into the well and does not diffuse into buffer; (2) bromophenol blue (migrates at ~300 bp equivalent) and xylene cyanol (~4 kb equivalent) for visible tracking of migration progress.
Molecular Sieving
Click to reveal
The physical process by which a porous matrix separates molecules by size. In gel electrophoresis, smaller DNA fragments navigate through gel pores more easily than larger ones. Large fragments experience greater resistance from multiple simultaneous pore interactions, causing them to migrate more slowly.
TAE Buffer
Click to reveal
Tris-Acetate-EDTA buffer. Standard buffer for agarose DNA gel electrophoresis. Tris buffers pH to 8.0–8.3. Acetate provides ionic conductivity. EDTA chelates Mg²⁺ to inhibit DNase activity and protect DNA during the run. The same buffer fills both the gel and the chamber.
SDS-PAGE
Click to reveal
Sodium Dodecyl Sulphate-Polyacrylamide Gel Electrophoresis. SDS is an anionic detergent that binds to proteins proportionally to chain length, coating them with a uniform negative charge proportional to their mass. This removes differences in native protein charge/shape, so separation is ONLY by molecular weight.

Section 15

📖 Glossary

Electrophoresis
Movement of charged molecules through a medium under an electric field. Negatively charged DNA migrates towards the positive electrode (anode). The rate of migration depends on the molecule's size, charge, and the resistance of the medium.
Agarose
A linear polysaccharide extracted from red algae, used as the gel matrix in DNA/RNA electrophoresis. Forms a porous network when solidified from hot solution. Pore size is controlled by agarose concentration.
Anode
The positive (+) electrode in an electrophoresis system. Negatively charged DNA migrates towards the anode. Located at the end of the gel farthest from the sample wells.
Cathode
The negative (−) electrode. DNA sample wells are always at the cathode end — DNA is loaded here and migrates away towards the anode when the electric field is applied.
DNA Ladder
A molecular weight standard containing a mixture of DNA fragments of precisely known sizes. Run alongside unknown samples in gel electrophoresis to provide a reference for size estimation by comparison of migration distances.
Gel Matrix
The porous network (agarose or polyacrylamide) through which molecules migrate in electrophoresis. Acts as a molecular sieve — resistance to migration increases with molecule size, enabling size-based separation.
Migration
Movement of DNA/RNA/protein through the gel under the applied electric field. Migration distance is inversely proportional to the log of fragment size: smaller fragments migrate farther than larger ones in the same time.
Staining
Process of making separated molecules visible after electrophoresis. For DNA: ethidium bromide (EtBr) intercalates into DNA and fluoresces under UV. Safer alternatives: SYBR Green, GelRed. For proteins: Coomassie Blue, silver stain.
Loading Dye
Solution mixed with DNA before loading into wells. Contains glycerol or Ficoll (increases density, keeps sample in well) and tracking dyes (bromophenol blue ≈ 300 bp; xylene cyanol ≈ 4 kb) for visible monitoring of migration.
Ethidium Bromide
Fluorescent dye that intercalates between DNA base pairs. Fluoresces bright orange under UV light (302 nm), making DNA bands visible. Detects ~1–10 ng DNA per band. Potent mutagen and carcinogen — safer alternatives include SYBR Green and GelRed.
