Gene Cloning: Steps from Gene to Clone

Memorise the eight-step sequence and what each step achieves.

Fig. 1. Flow chart of gene cloning.

Ligation basics

T4 DNA ligase joins 5′-phosphate to 3′-OH. Sticky ends ligate efficiently at 12–16 °C overnight; blunt ends need more enzyme and are helped by PEG. Dephosphorylating the vector with alkaline phosphatase reduces self-ligation.

PG focus

Compare restriction cloning with TA cloning, Gateway (att-site recombination) and Gibson assembly (exonuclease, polymerase, ligase one-pot).

📋 Summary

Summary & Exam Revision

Key points and model answers likely to appear in university examinations.

Key exam points
1
Cloning: isolate, cut, ligate, transform, select, screen, verify, grow.
2
Transformation (bacteria), transfection (animal cells), transduction (virus), microinjection, electroporation, liposome fusion.
3
Selection = survivors only; screening = tell clones apart.
4
Insertional inactivation: recombinant is Amp-R Tet-S when inserted in tet.
5
Blue-white: lacZα, X-gal, IPTG; white = recombinant.
6
Colony hybridisation uses a labelled probe on a membrane lift of colonies.
7
Sanger: ddNTP terminators; read from shortest to longest fragment.
8
PCR: denature 94–95, anneal 50–65, extend 72 °C; 2n amplification.
9
Southern DNA, Northern RNA, Western protein.
Short-answer questions

Click a question to reveal the model answer.

Q.1List the steps of gene cloning.▼

Isolate target DNA and vector; cut with restriction enzyme; ligate; introduce into host; select transformants; screen for recombinants; verify; grow and express.

Q.2Differentiate transformation, transfection and transduction.▼

Transformation: uptake of naked DNA by bacteria/yeast. Transfection: non-viral DNA into animal cells. Transduction: gene transfer by a virus.

Q.3Explain blue-white screening.▼

The vector carries lacZα in its MCS. Without insert, α-complementation makes active β-galactosidase which cleaves X-gal to blue. Insert disrupts lacZα, so colonies are white.

Q.4How does replica plating identify recombinants?▼

Colonies from a master ampicillin plate are replicated on amp + tet. Colonies absent on the second plate have lost tet through insertional inactivation, so they carry inserts.

Q.5State the principle of Sanger sequencing.▼

DNA polymerase extends a primer; occasional incorporation of ddNTPs (no 3′-OH) terminates chains. Fragments of all lengths are separated by size and the terminal base read.

Q.6Write the three steps of a PCR cycle with temperatures.▼

Denaturation 94–95 °C; annealing 50–65 °C; extension 72 °C (Taq).

Q.7Why does DNA migrate to the anode in electrophoresis?▼

The phosphate backbone carries a negative charge, so DNA moves toward the positive electrode; mobility decreases with log of size.

📖 Glossary

Key Terms & Glossary

Important terms in simple language.

Ligation
Joining DNA ends with ligase.
Competent cell
Cell treated to take up DNA.
Selection
Process allowing only vector-containing cells to grow.
Screening
Process that identifies cells with insert among survivors.
Replica plating
Transfer of colony pattern from one plate to others.
X-gal
Chromogenic substrate cleaved by β-galactosidase.
IPTG
Non-metabolisable inducer of the lac operon.
ddNTP
Dideoxynucleotide that terminates DNA synthesis.
Amplicon
Product of PCR amplification.
Ct value
Cycle number at which fluorescence crosses threshold in qPCR.
Probe
Labelled nucleic acid or antibody used for detection.
Anode
Positive electrode.
